# The dancer's silhouette

How do you recognize a waltz? Not by the color of the dress. You
watch the silhouette — the envelope of motion — and the dance
identifies itself regardless of who is wearing what.

Word problems are dances in costume. "Maria has three times as many
apples as Ben" and "the reservoir holds triple what the tank does"
are the same dance: one quantity, multiplied, equaling another. The
costume — names, objects, phrasing — is infinitely varied. The dance
is one of a small number of shapes.

The recognition half of our machine is built entirely around this
distinction. When we looked inside the network's own signals, the
split was measurably there: one part of the state carries the
**envelope** — what structural move the sentence is making — and
another carries the **carrier** — which particular words made it.
The head reads a sentence the way you watch a dancer: it segments
the motion into moves and classifies each move, and the costume is
deliberately destroyed on the way through.

The destruction is the point, and it has an address: a
**512-dimension waist**. Every token's representation — 2048 numbers
wide when the frozen language model hands it over — is forced
through this narrow channel before any reasoning touches it. This is
the information bottleneck of Tishby's information theory made into
architecture: understanding is not the preservation of information
but the *selective destruction* of it. Kill the phrasing, the names,
the order of telling; keep the quantities, the roles, the relations.
If two sentences mean the same thing, the waist should make them
*become* the same thing.

And the waist is not a gate you pass once. Deliberation lives inside
it — every cycle of the machine's thinking reads and writes through
the same narrow channel, so the pressure toward silhouette never
lets up. By the time a problem reaches the solver, there is no
costume left. There is only the dance, drawn in its barest lines —
and exact machinery can grade a bare line.

One consequence we did not fully anticipate: silhouettes are what
make honesty cheap. Our certification wall re-reads every problem
with sentences permuted and surface details shuffled — five costumes
on the same dance. A model that memorized costumes fails this
instantly. A model that sees silhouettes doesn't notice the change.
The waist, built for understanding, turned out to be the foundation
of trust.
